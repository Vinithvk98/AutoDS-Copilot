How it works

Five steps, and you approve each one.

There is no black box. At every stage you see what it found and decide what happens next.

01

Load

Point it at a CSV. It reads the structure and quietly fixes the obvious mess.

02

Explore

Clear charts with the code behind them, and checks that flag leakage and ID columns.

03

Clean

It proposes how to handle missing values and messy columns, and waits for your yes.

04

Model

It cross validates the candidates, balances the classes, and trains the best pick.

05

Understand

Metrics, an explained result, a dashboard, and a model you can download.

The copilot

It talks, reasons, and reads your data.

A grounded AI layer runs through the whole tool, and it can drive the run for you when you want.

Ask your data

Ask in plain language and it computes the answer from your real rows, with the calculation shown.

Grounded, never guessed

Every answer is built from your numbers and streamed as it writes. It says plainly when it does not know.

Imbalance, handled honestly

It rebalances the training data only, shows the before and after, tunes the threshold, and checks fairness.

Explained, not just scored

It shows which columns the model relies on, where it errs, and writes a short executive summary.

An agent that plans

Ask it to run the data and it cross validates, picks the best model, and proposes a full plan you approve.

Your model or none

Runs fully offline with no key, or connects to OpenAI, Claude, or a local model. Keys stay with you.

Built for trust

It works out the problem, and refuses to fool you.

The unglamorous half of machine learning, the part that decides whether a result is safe to act on, is where most of the care went.

No leakage

Preprocessing is fit on the training split only, inside the pipeline.

Honest imbalance

Rebalance train only, tune the threshold, check calibration.

Fairness checks

Error and selection rates per group, so bias is surfaced.

Real evaluation

Cross validation and a held out test at its true ratio.

On the churn data the honest answer was not the fashionable one. Gradient boosting usually wins, but a naive Bayes model scored higher here, and the agent said so, with the number to back it.
