In short

This is an analysis of sample_customers.csv. The file has 600 rows and 7 columns, 5 of them numeric and 2 holding categories.

The question is about churned, and AutoDS treated it as a classification problem. The outcomes are lopsided, one is much rarer than the other, so the scores below are chosen to stay honest when that happens.

What the data looks like

3 columns had gaps in them. The emptiest was income, missing about 8.0 percent of its values. Rather than quietly fill those gaps, AutoDS left them visible while exploring, so the charts show the real picture, and handled them safely later inside the model.

The file arrived clean, so nothing mechanical needed fixing before the analysis.

What we found

A few things stood out while exploring the data.

  • 3 column(s) have missing data; 'income' is worst at 8.0%.
  • Target is imbalanced — '0' is 87% of rows; consider class weighting or resampling.

How we chose a model

AutoDS did not settle for one guess. It cross checked every candidate on your data and ranked them by f1_weighted. Gaussian NB came out on top, ahead of Ada Boost Classifier and Logistic Regression.

How well it does

On data it had never seen, Gradient Boosting Classifier got the answer right about 84 percent of the time, with a balanced score of 0.804 that accounts for both kinds of mistake. It does a fair job of separating the two outcomes, with a ranking score of 0.694.

When it makes a call, it leans most on income, age, and tenure years. Those are the levers worth paying attention to.
